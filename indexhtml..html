HUNZA VALlEY

Hunza Valley, nestled in the heart of Gilgit Baltistan, Pakistan, is often called heaven on earth. Surrounded by the towering peaks of Rakaposhi, Ultar Sar , and ladyfinger, with the Hunza River flowing through apricot orchards and ancient forts like Baltit and Altit watching from above, its beauty is unmatched. But Hunza is more than just scenery — it is famous for its incredibly hospitable people, their long life expectancy, high literacy rate, and a peaceful, progressive culture that leaves every visitor with a feeling of serenity and wonder. BADSHAHi MOSQUE

The Badshahi Mosque in Lahore is one of the grandest symbols of Mughal splendor in Pakistan. Built in 1673 by Emperor Aurangzeb, it is the largest mosque of the Mughal era and the second-largest mosque in Pakistan. Made of red sandstone with delicate white marble inlay, its massive courtyard can hold over 100,000 worshippers, and its four towering minarets stand guard over the old city. Facing the historic Lahore Fort and the Minar-e-Pakistan, it is not just a place of worship, but a timeless masterpiece of Islamic architecture and Lahore's proud identity.

MAZAR E QUID

Mazar-e-Quaid, located in the heart of Karachi, is the final resting place of the Founder of Pakistan, Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah. This iconic white marble mausoleum, with its striking dome and four arches, stands as a symbol of national pride and unity. Surrounded by lush green gardens spread over 53 acres, it is not just a tomb, but a place where Pakistanis come to pay respect to the leader who gave them a homeland. Illuminated at night, the Mazar shines as a beacon of freedom and the enduring vision of Pakistan.

What a magnificent symbol of pride and freedom the white marble Mazar-e-Quaid is for every Pakistani!
